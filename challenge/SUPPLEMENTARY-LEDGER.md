# Supplementary ledger: the challenger reconciled at row grain

Generated by `python3 challenge/build_supplementary_ledger.py` into
`challenge/supplementary_ledger.csv`. Every count below recomputes from that file.

This **supersedes the company-grain comparison** in `challenge/reconcile.py`, which was a
deliberate weakening and turned out to be too coarse to adjudicate anything. Company presence
answers "did both datasets see this employer". It cannot answer "does this record exist on both
sides, and where they disagree, which one is right", and that is the only question that changes a
census.

No dashes are used as punctuation in this file.

## Why row grain was available all along

The workbook's `Thread/Dedup Key` is `Company + Role + Date`: 353 values, zero duplicates. That is
a record identifier. It joins to `company_canonical + role_as_listed + date_applied` on this side.
The first pass did not use it, and the cost was real: the company-grain reading reported "20
companies only in the workbook" where row grain finds **3 records** this corpus genuinely lacks.

## The ledger is a full outer join

Every workbook record gets a row. Every repository record with no workbook counterpart gets a row
too. A reconciliation that walks one side only measures what the challenger found and stays silent
on what it missed, which is how the first pass came to lead with where this repository won.

| Side | Rows |
|---|---|
| Workbook records | 353 |
| Repository records, census plus opportunity register | 329 |
| Ledger rows total | 406 |

## Matching, and where it refuses

Four ordered tiers, stopping at the first producing exactly one candidate. A tier returning more
than one stops the cascade and resolves to `ambiguous` with every candidate named, on the census
matcher's own rule: a refusal you cannot find later is not conservative, it is an unrecorded merge.

| Match tier | Rows |
|---|---|
| `exact_company_role_date` | 243 |
| `company_and_unspecified_role` | 29 |
| `company_only` | 17 |
| `company_and_role` | 10 |
| `ambiguous_at_exact_company_role_date` | 4 |
| `no_company_match` | 50 |

The 50 with no company match are almost entirely opportunity-register rows, addressed below.

## The four that actually need a decision

### `open_workbook_only`, 3 records

| Company | Role | Workbook category |
|---|---|---|
| AnyInt AI | Founding Sales (fully remote) | LinkedIn application |
| Meshy | Business Development Manager | Direct application / ATS |
| Pin.com | Outbound Business Development | Direct application / ATS |

AnyInt AI is already an open thread in `knowledge/02-current.md`, recorded before the workbook
arrived: a recruiter references an application and no receipt was ever found. The workbook
independently holding it raises the prior that the application is real, and still supplies no
artifact. All three stay out until one exists.

This number was **20 companies** under company-grain matching. Seventeen of those were matching
failures, not coverage gaps.

### `open_ambiguous`, 4 records

Two Attentive rows and two FOSSA rows, each matching both cycles of a genuine re-application. The
workbook does not carry a cycle component, so it cannot say which. Refused rather than guessed.

This is the third independent place the same two pairs surface: the duplicate detector clears them
as licensed second cycles, the platform matcher refuses to name a parent for them, and now the
supplementary ledger refuses too. Three mechanisms, one shape of data, consistent behaviour.

### `register_disagreement`, 7 records

Glytec, Mercor twice, SmartMode AI, WorkOS, micro1 twice. Each is a process the workbook counts and
this repository holds in the opportunity register, because no submission artifact establishes that
the applicant applied. WorkOS is the case already written up in `paper/DEFECTS.md`.

These are not errors on either side. They are the two-register rule doing exactly what it exists to
do, and they are the clearest measure of how far the workbook's 353 sits from a census: **46 plus 7
of its records can never be application-register rows here**, before any question of evidence.

### `declined_outside_evidence_title`, 27 records

The workbook names a role title where this census carries `unspecified`, from routes such as a
contemporaneous job newsletter, a same-period LinkedIn job recommendation, or an archived listing.

Each establishes that the company had an opening by that name in that period. None establishes
which opening the application was for, and a company running two openings makes the inference wrong
with nothing in the data showing it.

Contrast with the 9 marked `adopted_freeze31_title`, which came from platform applied-list rows.
Those record a specific submission rather than a nearby posting, which is the whole distinction.

**This is the open question for the author.** Adopting all 27 as a marked lower tier would take
`role_as_listed = unspecified` from 29 to single figures and move more rows into
`explicit_gtm_engineering`. It would also put inferred titles inside a census whose claim to rigor
is that every field traces to an artifact. The ledger keeps them visible and unadopted so the
decision stays open rather than being made by default.

## What the workbook missed, at row grain

`repository_only_census`, 41 records, of which **35 carry Tier A employer artifacts** and 6 Tier B.
Company-grain matching reported 27. Row grain finds more, because a company present in both can
still be missing a specific application on one side.

Neither reconstruction is complete and the union is larger than either. That statement now has a
row-level count behind it rather than a company-level estimate.

## A matcher defect this ledger found

Exa Labs was reported `open_workbook_only` while `exa|growth-lead|c1` sat in the census.
`company_key` strips legal suffixes but stops when the result would fall below four characters, so
"Exa Labs Inc." reduces to `exalabs` and never reaches `exa`. Prefix equivalence then refuses,
because it requires the shorter key to be at least four characters.

Two fixes, both in `challenge/reconcile.py`: `exalabs` is aliased to `exa`, restating a merge
`adjudication/ADJUDICATION.md` already recorded, and the alias table is now consulted **after**
suffix stripping as well as before, because stripping can produce a form the raw lookup never sees.

That is one row recovered and a class of failure closed. Any canonical company name under four
characters was previously unmatchable against its own longer form.

## What this ledger does not do

- **It does not adopt anything on its own.** Freeze 3 and Freeze 3.1 adoptions were made
  separately, with artifacts, and are logged in `knowledge/protocol.md`. The ledger records them as
  dispositions so they are visible in one place.
- **It does not compute an agreement statistic.** The workbook saw a different source set, so it is
  not a third blind coder, and 257 of 353 agreeing is a coverage fact rather than a reliability
  measure.
- **It does not resolve the outcome model.** The workbook's GHOSTED covers 252 of its 353 records
  and its own defect register calls that an overloaded residual. Comparing it row by row against
  `terminal_outcome` would compare a residual to an observation.
