# Supplementary ledger: the challenger reconciled at row grain

Generated by `python3 challenge/build_supplementary_ledger.py` into
`challenge/supplementary_ledger.csv`. Every count below recomputes from that file.

Reconciled against **`combined_job_search_audit_checkpoint_refined.xlsx`, 343 records**, sha256
`d32c869a...`. The earlier 353-record version is superseded; where a number moved between the two
workbook versions, this file carries the later one.

This **supersedes the company-grain comparison** in `challenge/reconcile.py`, which was a
deliberate weakening and turned out to be too coarse to adjudicate anything. Company presence
answers "did both datasets see this employer". It cannot answer "does this record exist on both
sides, and where they disagree, which one is right", and that is the only question that changes a
census.

No dashes are used as punctuation in this file.

## Why row grain was available all along

The workbook's `Thread/Dedup Key` is `Company + Role + Date`, with zero duplicates. That is a
record identifier. It joins to `company_canonical + role_as_listed + date_applied` on this side.
The first pass did not use it, and the cost was real: the company-grain reading reported "20
companies only in the workbook" where row grain finds **5 records** this corpus genuinely lacks.

## The ledger is a full outer join

Every workbook record gets a row. Every repository record with no workbook counterpart gets a row
too. A reconciliation that walks one side only measures what the challenger found and stays silent
on what it missed, which is how the first pass came to lead with where this repository won.

| Side | Rows |
|---|---|
| Workbook records | 343 |
| Repository records, census plus opportunity register | 351 |
| Ledger rows total | 405 |

than one stops the cascade and resolves to `ambiguous` with every candidate named, on the census
matcher's own rule: a refusal you cannot find later is not conservative, it is an unrecorded merge.

## The four that actually need a decision

### `open_workbook_only`, 5 records

| Company | Role | Workbook category |
|---|---|---|
| AnyInt AI | Founding Sales (fully remote) | LinkedIn application |
| Hotglue | Role title unresolved | Direct application / ATS |
| Meshy | Business Development Manager | Direct application / ATS |
| Mixmax | GTM Engineer (contract) | Community origins |
| Pinn | Outbound Business Development role (exact title unresolved) | Community origins |

AnyInt AI is already an open thread in `knowledge/02-current.md`, recorded before the workbook
arrived: a recruiter references an application and no receipt was ever found. The workbook
independently holding it raises the prior that the application is real, and still supplies no
artifact. All five stay out until one exists.

Hotglue and Pinn are new in the refined workbook and both carry an interview in its register.
Hotglue's stated origin is YC Work at a Startup, which is **unmet stop condition 6**: this study
never retrieved that source. That is the honest reading of the gap. It is not that the workbook
invented a row, it is that this corpus never looked where the row lives.

This number was **20 companies** under company-grain matching. Most of those were matching
failures, not coverage gaps.

### `open_ambiguous`, 4 records

Two Attentive rows and two FOSSA rows, each matching both cycles of a genuine re-application. The
workbook does not carry a cycle component, so it cannot say which. Refused rather than guessed.

This is the third independent place the same two pairs surface: the duplicate detector clears them
as licensed second cycles, the platform matcher refuses to name a parent for them, and now the
supplementary ledger refuses too. Three mechanisms, one shape of data, consistent behaviour.

### `register_disagreement`, 7 records

Glytec, Mercor twice, SmartMode AI, WorkOS, micro1 twice. Each is a process the workbook counts and
this repository holds in the opportunity register, because no submission artifact establishes that
the applicant applied. WorkOS is the case already written up in `paper/DEFECTS.md`.

These are not errors on either side. They are the two-register rule doing exactly what it exists to
do, and they are the clearest measure of how far the workbook's 343 sits from a census: **45 plus 7
of its records can never be application-register rows here**, before any question of evidence.

The refined workbook has moved toward this position rather than away from it. It now carries an
`Interview & Opportunity Register` sheet with an `Included in 343 App Ledger?` column, which is the
two-register split under a different name. The disagreement is no longer about whether the
distinction exists. It is about where seven specific rows fall.

### `declined_outside_evidence_title`, 17 records

The workbook names a role title where this census carries `unspecified`, from routes such as a
contemporaneous job newsletter, a same-period LinkedIn job recommendation, or an archived listing.

Each establishes that the company had an opening by that name in that period. None establishes
which opening the application was for, and a company running two openings makes the inference wrong
with nothing in the data showing it.

Contrast with the 9 marked `adopted_freeze31_title`, which came from platform applied-list rows.
Those record a specific submission rather than a nearby posting, which is the whole distinction.

**This is the open question for the author.** Adopting all 17 as a marked lower tier would take
`role_as_listed = unspecified` from 29 into single figures and move more rows into
`explicit_gtm_engineering`. It would also put inferred titles inside a census whose claim to rigor
is that every field traces to an artifact. The ledger keeps them visible and unadopted so the
decision stays open rather than being made by default.

This count was 27 against the 353-record workbook. Ten of those were adopted at Freeze 3.1 or
matched exactly once the refined workbook corrected its own company normalization.

## What the workbook missed, at row grain

`repository_only_census`, 50 records, of which **41 carry Tier A employer artifacts** and 9 Tier B.
Company-grain matching reported 27 companies. Row grain finds more, because a company present in
both can still be missing a specific application on one side.

A further 12 are `repository_only_non_census`, split 6 application-register rows the workbook never
saw and 6 opportunity-register rows outside its scope.

Neither reconstruction is complete and the union is larger than either. That statement now has a
row-level count behind it rather than a company-level estimate.

## Where the refined workbook regressed

It removed **eight receipts** because it could not classify their role. Six of the companies behind
them are named in the clear in its own defect register, and **all six are in this census**:

| Company | Role here | Evidence tier | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hightouch | Go-to-Market Engineer | A | 2026-07-15 |
| MediaLab.AI Inc. | unspecified | A | 2026-01-02 |
| Blackthorn.io | unspecified | A | 2025-08-05 |
| Proofpoint | unspecified | A | 2026-01-02 |
| Designit | unspecified | A | 2025-07-08 |
| Lumenalta | unspecified | B | 2026-03-24 |

Five carry Tier A employer artifacts. Hightouch carries a named role of Go-to-Market Engineer,
which is the exact field the workbook says it could not resolve.

This is the same anti-pattern the challenge names in `challenge/CHALLENGE.md`: **a field the
schema cannot resolve should never gate inclusion of the row.** The workbook fixed its origin
gating between versions and reintroduced the identical failure on role title. An unresolved title
is a reason to write `unspecified`, which is what this census does on 29 rows. It is not a reason
to drop the receipt.

## A scope disagreement, not an error

The refined workbook removes four school-district and substitute-teaching rows as "false population
records". That is a defensible scope rule, and it is a genuine disagreement rather than a defect on
either side.

It is also larger than the workbook's own edit implies. Applied consistently to this census the
rule reaches **15 rows**, 14 of them Tier A:

| Company | Rows |
|---|---|
| DeKalb County School District | 10 |
| Atlanta Public Schools | 1 |
| City Schools Of Decatur | 1 |
| Douglas County School System | 1 |
| Stellar Substitute | 1 |
| Mercor, Education / school Evaluator | 1 |

None of the 15 is in the interview set. Removing them all moves the application census from 223 to
**208** and the headline rate from 11/223 to **11/208**, which raises the interview rate rather
than lowering it, because the removed rows are all non-interviewing.

Whether substitute-teaching applications belong in the denominator of a **GTM engineering** job
search is a real question and it is the author's to answer, not the ledger's. It is recorded here
unresolved. No figure in the paper assumes either answer, and the paper reports 11/223 with this
sensitivity stated.

## What this ledger does not do

- **It does not adopt anything on its own.** Freeze 3 and Freeze 3.1 adoptions were made
  separately, with artifacts, and are logged in `knowledge/protocol.md`. The ledger records them as
  dispositions so they are visible in one place.
- **It does not compute an agreement statistic.** The workbook saw a different source set, so it is
  not a third blind coder, and 256 of 343 agreeing is a coverage fact rather than a reliability
  measure.
- **It does not resolve the outcome model.** The workbook's GHOSTED still covers the large majority
  of its records and its own defect register calls that an overloaded residual. Comparing it row by
  row against `terminal_outcome` would compare a residual to an observation.
